Race Weekend Abidjan 2026

Two days built around a live giant screen projection of the Monaco Grand Prix. One of the first premium Formula 1 experiences staged in Côte d’Ivoire.

The concept

Formula 1, brought to Abidjan.

Formula 1 has a real and growing audience in Côte d’Ivoire, but until Race Weekend there was no premium space in which to experience it. The passion existed; the venue did not.

AToure built one. Race Weekend took over Espace Superkart in Abidjan across 6 and 7 June 2026 and turned the Monaco Grand Prix into a destination across two days: live projection on a giant screen, a karting circuit running alongside it, a supercar paddock, a VIP lounge overlooking the track, and 300 guests a day drawn from the city’s young professional and motorsport communities.

The result was less a screening than a weekend: sport, lifestyle, networking and content, designed as a single experience.

The format

Two days. Two different rooms.

Race Weekend was structured so that each day earned its own audience. The first built around the track and the machinery, the second around the race itself.

Why it worked

First mover, on purpose.

Formula 1’s global fanbase is young, digital and premium. A profile that maps almost exactly onto Abidjan’s emerging professional class. Roughly 42% of F1 fans are under 35, and the sport’s official channels carry an audience in the tens of millions.

No one had built a premium F1 activation in Côte d’Ivoire before. Race Weekend claimed that space first, and did it in the 2026 season, which carried unusual pull: a new technical regulation, new manufacturers entering the grid, and a field mixing established names with a new generation of drivers.

The opportunity

  • Young audience~42% of F1 fans are under 35
  • Global scaleF1’s official channels reach tens of millions
  • Open marketNo premium F1 activation in Côte d’Ivoire to date
  • TimingA 2026 season with new rules and new manufacturers
  • Cultural pullF1 as lifestyle and business, not only sport

Built to be lived, photographed and shared

Premium welcome, sponsor wall, signature cocktails and a live Grand Prix on the big screen downstairs; VIP lounge, coffee experience, DJ set and track views upstairs. Every touchpoint was designed to work as an experience first and as content immediately after.

Filmed, hosted, covered.

Media and coverage

Both days were produced as content from the outset: a dedicated photo and video team on site, hosted segments and interviews from the floor, and press and digital media accredited across the weekend.

The output ran from editorial photography and creator reels through to official productions and backstage coverage, giving partners assets that lived well beyond the two days themselves.

Two accounts were built for the event and run through the weekend: @race.weekend for the audience and @race.mediaciv for the media desk. Both started from nothing.

Press and platforms

Who picked it up.

Race Weekend was covered by Ivorian and panafrican media, by the broadcast partner, and by the country’s events and lifestyle pages. These are the outlets carrying the event publicly.

Scoop Afrique

Panafrican digital news. Reported the weekend as a success across TikTok, Instagram and Facebook, with the TikTok cut passing 12,700 likes and framing the venue as Abidjan living to the rhythm of the Monaco Grand Prix.

  • Seven creators, 3.9M combined following
